## Guidelines for the Assignment:  FINAL 

This Mapping Assignment, otherwise known as Assignment 2, will be completed in one step. It builds on work we did in class, particularly with annotating "1, the Road" by Ross Goodwin with the web-based semantic annotation tool [Recogito](https://recogito.pelagios.org/). 

> Manual geocoding is essentially a human identification and contextualization of placenames found in a text and matching them with possible geographic information using contextual clues. Automatic geocoding is based on a machine identifying possible place names through two predictive processes (1) assigning certain words the tag of geographic identity and (2) matching those against databases without contextualization.

## Guiding Questions (you do not need to answer all these questions):

**Observations**: 
- Do you observe any patterns when comparing the manually and automatically geocoded locations? between the two different automatically geocoded locations? 
- How did the different groups annotate differently. 
- Do the two methods produce similar coordinates or mappings?
- Where do the largest discrepancies occur? At different scales? kinds of places?

**Agreement/disagreement of the two methods**:
- Identify one or two cases where manual and automatic geocoding differ significantly. What is happening in those examples?
- How different are the human annotations in different sections made by different annotators?
- Are mismatches random, or do they follow patterns (e.g., ambiguous place names, incomplete data)?
- Are certain types of locations (cities, regions, historical places, commercial locations) more prone to disagreement? 
- Optional: take a look at the NER processes with in the two scripts using spaCy. What are its assumptions about place names and place name extraction? What is the gazetteer used by geopy to get latitude/longitude pairs? Can you find out any information about it?  

**Data and Sensitivity**: 
- How does the quality or completeness of the original place names affect each geocoding method?
- What happens when place names are: misspelled, have OCR errors, are too granular or vague (e.g., “near Atlanta”).

**Interpretation vs automation**: 
- What kinds of decisions are humans making in manual geocoding that automated systems cannot easily replicate?
- What assumptions does the automated system make when matching place names to coordinates?
- Can you think of a case when automatic geocoding might have significant implications?
- How does each method handle ambiguity (e.g., multiple places with the same name)?
- Comment on the multiple levels of automation that take place here. Do they help us or hinder us in understanding "human space"?
- How does color and/or jitter help make the map more visible? 

**Critical Questions**: 
- What kinds of places or regions might be systematically misrepresented by automated geocoding systems?
- How did Goodwin's creation of data by "Wordcar"--including pulling information from the Foursquare API--create a certain kind of data?
- What does this comparison reveal about the limits of computational approaches to spatial data?
- In the video and introduction, they mention that the car produced many thousand words. How long is the novel? Can studying the map help us to understand anything about how much of the automatically created text was trimmed by the editor?
