## Overview - DRAFT 

I have curated here a number of possible oral exam questions that would be relevant for the end of term in this class. 

## Format of the Oral Exam 

The oral exams will cover readings, material covered in your assignments and any material, from the entire semester, but more specifically in the class since the midterm written exam. 

The oral exam will take place in person in my office. Everyone has signed up for a time to have their exam. It will last a maximum of 20 minutes (expect a little less). There are 10 minutes between each of the oral exams of downtime for me. Please be on time, but don't come so early so that you wait outside my office. If late, you will only have the remainder of the time left for the meeting.

The most important part of the Oral Exam is not all the knowledge you have memorized, but that it is a "student-driven" conversation. I will expect for you to keep the conversation going. I will ask follow up questions, time permitting.

## Rationale behind having an Oral Exam

- to put the course skills in a larger context
- to give students an opportunity to synthesize course materials periodically
- to use terms from the course correctly in academic conversation
- to see if you are able to revise or deepen an answer when prompted to go beyond an initial answer 
- to demonstrate metacognitive and reflective thinking ("I know this, but I'm not sure about...")
- to demonstrate awareness of course interests outside of the class, in a spoken academic register

> Sample question: "Tell me in your own words about the concept of X." 

## Level 2: Sample Questions about the Course Materials

- Which concept was your favorite from class? Why? 
- Which reading was your least favorite? Why? 
- What's one idea from the readings or classes you found intriguing? Why? 
- What's one idea from the readings or classes you disagree with? Why?
- Which element of thinking about data made the greatest impression on you? Why?
- Which genre of material do you like the best (podcast, essay, digital project)?
- What insights do you have from new technical skills have you been exposed to?
- Which tools do you imagine you will use after the course? Why?
- Which concepts presented in the course are the most challenging to understand? 

> NB: I will have a 'question bank' and so they will not be exactly the ones listed above. 

## LEVEL 3: Questions about Your Assignment 2 or 3

- What was the main distinction between human annotation and machine annotation (A2)?
- What did A2 reveal about the limits of computational approaches to spatial data?
- How did you choose the source that you worked with (A3)? 
- How did your prompting strategy change as a result of verification of the data (A3)? 
- What assumptions does the automated system make when matching place names to coordinates? (A2)
- What did you learn about the topic of your assignment that you didn't know before (A2/A3)? 
- Do you imagine that you will use computer/LLM-assisted approaches in the future to work with data? Why or why not? (A2/A3)
- What kinds of biases can we find embedded in data (A2/A3)?

> NB: I will have a 'question bank' and so they will not be exactly the ones listed above. 

## LEVEL 4: Questions for Reflecting on the Class more Generally

- What are you learning from this class that you have never been exposed to before? 
- What are you not sure about in the class that you would like to dive deeper into? 
- How do the materials of the class relate to your experiences? 
- What subject of the class have you told your friends about?
- If you were to take a follow up class related to this one, what would the topic be about? 

> NB: I will have a 'question bank' and so they will not be exactly the ones listed above.
