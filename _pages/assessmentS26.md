This course uses a diverse set of assessment methods to evaluate your learning and growth. Assessment is ongoing and formative, with opportunities for feedback and extra credit throughout the semester.

---

## Grading Components

### Class Participation (10%)

Active participation is essential for success in this course. This includes:
- Attending synchronous class sessions (TuTh 11:20-12:45)
- Engaging thoughtfully in discussions and activities
- Completing in-class exercises and workshops
- Contributing to peer learning and collaborative work

**Note:** Attendance is mandatory. If you do not attend class sessions, or stop attending classes for a sustained period, it will not be possible to complete coursework and receive higher than a C. Typically, 2-3 absences are tolerated over the course of the semester.

---

### Four Assignments (70% total — the first one is 10%; 30% for two counted)

There are four open-ended assignments that apply course methods to new contexts and texts. I will count three of them. It is your choice to complete three or four assignments. You can complete the fourth assignment for up to five (5) extra points on top of your course work. If you do not complete an assignment within ten days of the due date, you will not be allowed to go back to complete it. 

**Purpose:** To develop practical skills in humanities data with a geospatial element and to practice critical thinking and evaluation about technology and artefacts from the humanities. These responses allow you to reflect on conceptual issues raised in class, practice web-based academic writing, and develop a public scholarly voice. 

**Format and Length:**
- Typically 1,500-2,000 words, plus any needed visuals (maps, screenshots, charts, etc). Each assignment will be specific about what is required.
- Combines written analysis with digital outputs or demonstrations
- Published on your individual course Github page.
- Everyone will complete the first exercise alone. It will be specified in each of the subsequent assignments if you can do it alone or in pairs. Groups of three or more are not permitted. 

**Detailed instructions will be provided and rubrics will be linked to each of the assignments. 

### Oral Exams (20% total — 10% each)

The oral exams will cover readings, material covered in your assignments and any aspect more generally in the class. They will last 15 minutes each and will be in person. You will do the oral exam on your own. They will take place before the Fall break and in week 12.
